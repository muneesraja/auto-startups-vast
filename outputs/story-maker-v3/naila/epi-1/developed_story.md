# Naila and the Shoulder-Top View

In a lush green forest, little Naila lives with her father at an animal shelter they
run together. One drowsy afternoon Naila falls asleep on a wooden swing strung between
two trees — and a bad dream finds her. She wakes already crying, tears rolling down her
cheeks.

Her two companions try to help. Neju, the green parrot, flutters down to her shoulder
and gently taps her cheek with a wing. Azhagi, the golden dog, sits below the swing and
barks soft, worried little barks to cheer her. Nothing works — the crying goes on.

Azhagi decides to fetch help. The dog races off across the shelter yard, ears flying,
toward the far paddock where father is busy feeding the elephant. Azhagi skids to a
stop and barks urgently. Father looks up, understands at once, and swings onto his
horse.

Father rides back to the swing at a easy canter, the dog racing alongside. He draws
rein by Naila, leans down, and calls her name — warm and low. The crying stops as if
switched off; Naila's face breaks into a slow, wide smile.

Then father lifts her up and sets her on his shoulders. Her eyes go wide. From up
there she can see the whole shelter at once — the swing, the dog, the parrot circling,
the elephant by the trees, the green forest rolling away on every side. She is high
and safe and happy, looking out over her whole small world.
