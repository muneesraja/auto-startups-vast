# Coverage (situation then subject)

Camera grammar for Agents 3–5. Situation first, then subject. **One primary
subject** per shot. **One** MiniMax H3 camera sentence. Hold 180° / screen
direction.

A cut only if it adds information. If only the angle changes, move the camera
instead of cutting. Never write “cinematic” as the whole camera line. Do not
stack three moves in one shot.

## Situations

- **establish** — wide geography.
- **discover** — insert (object/animal/detail), then human reaction.
- **danger** — 2–4 shots: static or slow push, then reaction CU.
- **protect** — medium two-shot / OTS; adults as shield.
- **emotion** — MCU/CU on face + body.
- **scale** — humans small vs canopy / terrain.
- **play_comedy** — **only here** use 5–8 micro-shots per 15s generation.

## Subjects

**Humans.** Adult vs child eyeline. Reaction is CU/MCU on face and body.

**Animals.** Frame role + state from [`creature_behavior.md`](creature_behavior.md)
(stalk through cover, prey escape path, herd scatter). No zoo-portrait during
danger unless that shot is the reveal; the next shot is human reaction.

**Objects / product.** Insert CU only when looked at or used; match the looker
eyeline; then reaction. No idle orbits. Ads: pack/label is an object subject
(hero insert, then human reaction), not a spinning prop.

**Plants / trees / terrain.** Scale, cover, path. Plant CU only for `discover`.
No dutch-roll canopy montage.

## Agent 3 `camera:` recipe

One line: shot size + subject + situation + **one** H3 move with type +
amplitude + speed.

Examples: “MCU on the mother’s face, protect, Static Shot.” · “Wide, humans
small under the canopy, scale, slow Pull Out with small amplitude.” · “Product
hero, slow Push In with small amplitude at slow speed.”

Match move to action: tracking for travel, push-in for emotion, orbit for
product/reveal, wide swing for crowd, static/slow push for danger.

Ban subject-less whip pans and “different camera every panel.”
