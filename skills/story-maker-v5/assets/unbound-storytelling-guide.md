# Unbound Limitless Storytelling Guide

A master directing playbook for stripping away sanitized AI clichés, polite textbook prose, and generic stage directions. This guide elevates story scripts, character interactions, and video prompts into visceral, character-driven cinematic experiences.

---

## 1. The Core Philosophy: Eliminating Sanitized AI Fiction

Default LLM storytelling defaults to sanitized, explanatory prose:
- Characters act like polite mouthpieces explaining their feelings (*"I am frustrated because you took my turn"*).
- Emotions are labeled as abstract concepts (*"He was stubborn; she was authoritative"*).
- Conflicts resolve with tidy, moralizing after-school-special lessons.
- Environments are described like static real estate listings rather than lived-in, tactile spaces.

**The Unbound Standard** rejects this artificial polish. Stories must feel **visceral, physically alive, psychologically authentic, and stylistically bold**. Subtext is communicated through muscle tension, material friction, acoustic rhythm, and messy human behavior.

---

## 2. The 5 Pillars of Unbound Storytelling

### Pillar 1: Physicalizing Subtext (Anatomy Over Adjectives)
Never state what a character feels inside. Show how the physical world acts on their body and how their body reacts involuntarily to stimuli.

- **The Involuntary Reaction Chain:**
  1. **Stimulus:** A sound, sudden movement, impact, or visual surprise.
  2. **Freeze:** Muscular arrest for a split-second.
  3. **Ocular Micro-Beat:** Pupils dilate, eyelids twitch, or eyes lock onto a detail.
  4. **Breath & Jaw:** Sudden sharp inhalation, jaw clench, mouth parting, or puffed cheeks.
  5. **Postural Realignment:** Shifting center of gravity, digging boot heels in, ducking, or stepping back.
  6. **Secondary Material Friction:** Scuffed boots squeaking on steel plates, knuckles whitening around tin chassis, braids swinging over shoulders.

### Pillar 2: Authentic Vernacular & Elastic Dialogue Rhythm
Real dialogue is rapid, messy, colloquial, and rhythmic. People interrupt, clip their sentences, talk around issues, use regional slang, and weaponize humor.

- **Rules for Unbound Dialogue:**
  - **No Grammar Textbook Politeness:** In moments of action or conflict, characters use fragments, slang, and abrupt interruptions.
  - **Grounding in Cultural Voice:** Use authentic vernacular, affectionate idioms, and regional exclamations (e.g. South African *Eish!*, *Yoh!*, *Hau!*) that give characters living soul rather than sounding like translated subtitles.
  - **Subtextual Banter:** Characters rarely say what they mean directly; affectionate teasing, playful threats, and territorial squabbles reveal deep bonds far better than saccharine declarations.
  - **The Authority Arrival Pivot & Anti-Repetition Gate:** Characters must NEVER repeat the same blame or accusation across cuts (e.g. ban repeating "It leaked! / No, it flooded!"). When an authority figure (e.g. Caloo) enters, characters freeze, drop the fight, and pivot immediately to an appeal, bargaining, or plea (e.g. Ollie offering the wet helmet). The authority responds with knowing swagger ("I know what you were really doing down there"), instantly moving the story forward.

| :--- | :--- |
| *"Please return that to me, it is my invention."* | *"Ollie! Give it back! It's my helmet!" / "No! I built it!"* |
| *"Caloo entered: 'What did I say about the deep water?' Ollie: 'The fish started it!'" (repeated twice)* | *"Caloo enters: 'What did I say about the deep end?' Ollie drops the helmet, locks eyes with Caloo, and pleads: 'Dad... it still works, right?' Caloo smirks: 'I know what you were really doing down there.'"* |
| *"I agree that exploring together is a better choice."* | *"Ollie... this is way better than chores." / "Definitely. Now gimme that lily pad!"* |

### Pillar 3: Dynamic Pacing & Elastic Comic/Dramatic Tempo
A scene must breathe with rhythmic compression and release. If the tension stays flat, the audience checks out.

- **The Four-Beat Tempo Curve:**
  1. **Escalation (Fast & Frantic):** Rapid physical conflict, rising vocal volume, kinetic motion.
  2. **The Mechanical Break (Instant Shock):** An unexpected physical event—a loud CRACK, a dropped dish, a sudden door bang.
  3. **Dead Acoustic Pause (The Vacuum):** Absolute silence or faint background ambience (wind, dripping pipe) for 1–2 beats where characters freeze and process.
  4. **The Release (Comedic or Dramatic Explosion):** Simultaneous finger-pointing, shared laughter, or a theatrical entrance that shatters the silence.

---

### Pillar 4: Tactile Sensory Worldbuilding & Prop Ergonomics
A scene must engage at least two non-visual senses per beat. Ground the reader and the video generator in physical materials, temperatures, textures, and sounds.

- **Tactile Details:** Rough bark weave, slick moss on stone, sticky amber sap, cold glassy water, woven leaf straps, smooth polished pebble.
- **Olfactory & Gustatory Imagery:** Crushed clover, wet fur, sun-warmed bark, pondweed tang, green algae bloom.
- **Acoustic Muscle (Tactile Foley):** Squeaking paws on wet ledge, strained bark creak, hollow wooden thunk, muffled underwater hum, roaring splash, seismic snail-bloom chime.
- **Prop Ergonomics & Spatial Plausibility (MANDATORY):**
  - **Individual Vessels for Multi-Character Dining:** When multiple characters eat or use tools, allocate individual portions (e.g. two separate bark cups, one set before each Pookoo).
  - **Ban Shared-Bowl Cramming:** Never depict multiple characters eating simultaneously out of a single shared vessel—AI image and video models will distort anatomy, tangle limbs, and collapse into visual chaos.
  - Distinguish *Serving* (Caloo pouring from a waterskin) from *Drinking* (each Pookoo drinking from his own cup).

---

### Pillar 5: Anti-Moralizing Resolutions (Earned Warmth & Commercial Buttons)
Never end a story with a tidy moral lesson or characters declaring what they learned.

- **Show True Reconciliation Through Shared Behavior:**
  - Family love is proven when Ollie sheepishly offers his helmet-cup of pond water to Caloo, or Caloo flicks a droplet off Ollie's nose.
  - Paternal authority is proven through confident swagger, playful winks, and nourishing warmth, not lectures.
- **The 10-Second Commercial Button Formula (Branded Stories):**
  - When delivering a product slogan or commercial payoff, keep it punchy and authentic (10.0s total):
    - **Setup (2s):** Savoring aroma or locking eyes with the hero item.
    - **Authentic Slogan (5s):** Slogan delivered inside `<d>...</d>` with natural swagger (e.g. *"If your Pookoos are parched, give them the splash they're craving—Pondfresh!"*).
    - **Crunch Button (3s):** Loud, satisfying foley splash/crunch, beaming smile, and held brand tableau.

## 3. Workflow Integration for story-maker-v5 Agents

### Agent 1: Story Developer (`prompts/story_developer.md`)
- **Ban Emotion Labels:** Reject inputs or prose that rely on "felt angry," "was happy," "realized their bond."
- **Enforce Physical Stakes:** Every scene must have a physical obstacle, material damage potential, or tangible prize.
- **Character Quirks:** Ensure each character has a specific physical tick (e.g. nose twitching at food, adjusting forehead goggles, pouting with puffed cheeks).

### Agent 3: Storyboard Planner (`prompts/storyboard_planner.md`)
- **Compositional Asymmetry:** Avoid generic centered staging in every panel. Contrast tight, claustrophobic close-ups of straining hands with deep-focus wide angles of shelter interiors.
- **Visual Gags & Squash-and-Stretch:** Use classic comic acting—eyes widening into cartoon starbursts, exaggerated jaw drops, kinetic speed lines during violent snaps.

### Agent 5: Video Prompter (`prompts/video_prompter.md`)
- **Micro-Acting Chains in Director's Briefs:** Construct every shot around explicit physiological stages: `Stimulus -> Freeze -> Eye/Brow -> Posture Shift -> Secondary Motion`.
- **Sound Design Section:** Every `Audio:` line must specify tactile materials, acoustics, ambient room tone, and vocal delivery style.
- **Direct Address Commercial Charm:** When characters break the fourth wall for slogans or taglines, stage them with natural charm, holding hero props at waist or chest height with dynamic lighting catchlights.
